# Working Brand Charter

**Brand:** SoberSend  
**Subtitle:** A recovery lifestyle brand for living fully, together.  
**Status:** Working draft  
**Source:** `SoberSend_Brand_Charter_Working_Draft.docx`  
**Prepared from:** Founder discovery conversations with Matt Stitz

Founder vision, mission, community model, brand architecture, and strategic guardrails.

## Purpose of this document

Align brand, product, design, community, partnership, and legal work around a shared definition of what SoberSend is—and what it should become.

---

## 01 · Brand at a Glance

The shortest useful expression of the SoberSend idea.

> SoberSend is built on a simple belief: sobriety is not a smaller life. It is a chance to go all in.

### Working brand essence

| | |
|---|---|
| **Brand type** | Recovery lifestyle brand, community, and parent company for a portfolio of independently named products. |
| **Core idea** | "Send it" means go for it, give it everything, and live boldly. SoberSend brings that spirit to sober living and recovery. |
| **Mission** | Help people recover and grow together through identity, belonging, connection, adventure, service, and positive products. |
| **Vision** | Become a household name in the recovery community and, over time, an international symbol of proud, connected, fully lived sobriety. |
| **Primary audience** | People in recovery; people considering sobriety; people in active addiction seeking a path forward; sober-by-choice people; and supporters of sober living. |
| **Brand promise** | SoberSend will make sobriety feel like a launchpad for a fuller life—not a retreat from it. |
| **Near-term expression** | A live website, social presence, visible apparel, and a first portfolio of five to ten positive apps. |

### Working positioning statement

For people who are sober, seeking sobriety, or support sober living, SoberSend is a recovery lifestyle brand and community that turns sobriety into a source of identity, belonging, adventure, growth, and service. Unlike brands that frame recovery only around abstinence or struggle, SoberSend celebrates what becomes possible when people live fully and recover together.

The sections that follow translate this idea into a founder story, mission, community model, brand voice, product architecture, messaging platform, and practical roadmap.

## 02 · Origin & Founder Story

A brand rooted in lived experience rather than a manufactured market opportunity.

> My friends would say "send it" when they wanted to do something cool—jump off a cliff, ride a bike, go for it. It means give it all you've got. Because I'm sober and in recovery, I loved the idea of SoberSend: a brand for people in recovery to do amazing things, explore the world, and live with a sense of adventure.
>
> — **Matt Stitz, Founder**

Matt describes himself openly and proudly as a grateful alcoholic in recovery. He is active in the recovery community, does not hide his sobriety, and wants SoberSend to help as many people as possible. That personal credibility is foundational: SoberSend is not borrowing the language of recovery from the outside. It is being built from within the community, by someone whose life and service are already connected to it.

### The meaning inside the name

**SOBER**  
Presence, clarity, honesty, recovery, choice, and a life no longer organized around alcohol or drugs.

**SEND**  
Commitment, action, courage, adventure, momentum, and the willingness to go all in.

Together, the words create a productive tension. "Sober" can be misunderstood as restraint, deprivation, or a life made smaller. "Send" reverses that expectation. It says that recovery can be bold, joyful, social, physical, creative, adventurous, and deeply alive.

### Founder conviction

- Sobriety is something to wear proudly, not something to conceal.
- Recovery is stronger when people do it together rather than in isolation.
- People become better versions of themselves by learning from and emulating people they respect.
- A meaningful sober life includes adventure, connection, growth, service, and contribution.
- A brand can help make recovery feel socially magnetic, culturally relevant, and aspirational.

## 03 · Mission, Vision & Values

The internal compass for decisions, partnerships, products, and community behavior.

### Mission

Create a brand and community that people can identify with—one that helps people recover together, form meaningful connections, become better versions of themselves, embrace sobriety as a proud part of their identity, get out into the world, do amazing things, give back, and leave the world better than they found it.

### Vision

Make SoberSend a household name in the recovery community: a trusted, visible, and culturally resonant lifestyle brand that grows from the United States into an international community and product ecosystem.

### Core belief

> We recover in packs, not as isolated individuals.

*Community principle*

### Brand values

**Proud Recovery**  
Treat sobriety and recovery as a source of strength, honesty, and identity—not shame.

**Connection**  
Create relationships, shared experiences, and community strong enough to counter isolation.

**Adventure**  
Encourage people to explore, take healthy risks, try new things, and fully participate in life.

**Growth**  
Help people keep becoming—through practice, role models, reflection, challenge, and action.

**Service**  
Give back, help the next person, strengthen the pack, and leave people and places better.

**Optimism with Honesty**  
Acknowledge the reality of addiction and recovery without reducing people to struggle or trauma.

### Decision test

A SoberSend initiative should make it easier for people to feel proud, connected, energized, useful, and capable of living a fuller life. If it isolates, shames, exploits, glamorizes addiction, or treats recovery as merely a commercial aesthetic, it does not belong.

## 04 · Audience & Community Model

Recovery-centered, but intentionally broad enough to welcome people at different stages and with different relationships to sobriety.

### Primary audience

- People in recovery from alcohol or drug addiction, including those who identify as alcoholics or addicts.
- People considering sobriety or beginning to question their relationship with alcohol or drugs.
- People in active addiction who may be seeking hope, connection, identification, or a path toward change.
- People who choose a sober lifestyle without identifying as alcoholic or addicted.
- Friends, family members, allies, and others who actively support sober living and recovery.

### Community philosophy

SoberSend should create more than an audience. It should create identification and belonging. Members should be able to look at the brand, its people, and its experiences and think: "These are my people. This is a life I want. I can become more by doing this alongside them."

**Belonging before broadcasting**  
The community should not feel like followers gathered around a brand. It should feel like people finding one another through a shared identity and set of values.

**Role models over influencers**  
Elevate people whose lives demonstrate integrity, growth, adventure, service, creativity, and long-term recovery—not only reach or aesthetics.

**Participation over consumption**  
Invite people to meet, move, build, serve, learn, create, and contribute rather than only purchase or scroll.

**Broad welcome, clear center**  
Welcome sober-by-choice people and supporters while keeping lived recovery, addiction, and service at the moral center of the brand.

### How people should feel

| | |
|---|---|
| **Seen** | My experience is understood without being reduced to a diagnosis or stereotype. |
| **Proud** | Sobriety is an identity I can claim publicly and positively. |
| **Connected** | I am not doing this alone; I have people to learn from and people I can help. |
| **Activated** | Recovery opens the door to action, adventure, and contribution. |
| **Hopeful** | There are compelling lives, relationships, and futures available to me. |

## 05 · Positioning, Personality & Voice

How SoberSend should stand apart and show up consistently.

### Positioning territory

SoberSend sits at the intersection of recovery, lifestyle, adventure, community, personal growth, and service. It should not feel like clinical treatment, institutional recovery messaging, a novelty sobriety slogan, or a generic wellness brand with recovery language added later.

### Brand personality

**Bold, not reckless**  
Encourages healthy courage and full participation without romanticizing danger or impulsivity.

**Proud, not performative**  
Celebrates sobriety with authenticity and humility rather than self-congratulation.

**Warm, not sentimental**  
Builds belonging through honesty, humor, humanity, and shared experience.

**Inclusive, not diluted**  
Welcomes many paths to sober living while remaining grounded in recovery from addiction.

**Optimistic, not naive**  
Believes in possibility while respecting relapse, grief, struggle, and the work recovery requires.

**Commercial, not extractive**  
Can make money and grow, but must protect trust and never exploit vulnerability.

### Emotional shorthand

| Should feel | Should not feel |
|-------------|-----------------|
| Alive, welcoming, honest, adventurous, modern, grounded, useful, and connected. | Clinical, corporate, preachy, performative, reckless, exclusive, or exploitative. |

> SoberSend should feel like people you trust inviting you into a bigger life.

*Working experience standard*

### Voice principles

**Direct**  
Use plain language. Say what matters without jargon, euphemism, or corporate distance.

**Human**  
Sound like a real person in recovery speaking to another person—not a campaign or institution.

**Energizing**  
Point toward possibility, action, experiences, and contribution.

**Grounded**  
Earn optimism through honesty. Do not promise that recovery is easy or linear.

**Respectful**  
Let people name themselves. Avoid policing recovery paths or creating purity tests.

**Community-first**  
Use "we," "together," and concrete invitations to participate rather than treating people as an audience to monetize.

### Language guardrails

| Use more of | Use less of |
|-------------|-------------|
| Connection, service, adventure, pride, honesty, belonging, courage, growth, showing up, doing it together. | Shame, scare tactics, savior language, clinical claims, recovery superiority, empty inspiration, or glamorized chaos. |

## 06 · Brand Architecture & Business Model

SoberSend is the enduring parent and lifestyle brand; individual products can develop identities of their own.

> The brand and its mission are the center. The apps are products launched under that umbrella—not the definition of SoberSend.

*Founder direction*

### Recommended architecture

| Layer | Role |
|-------|------|
| **Parent brand** | SoberSend: the company, mission, community, lifestyle identity, apparel brand, and long-term source of trust. |
| **Apps/products** | Independently named sub-brands with their own identities. SoberSend may appear lightly as the owner or publisher, but not as prominent in-product branding. |
| **App portfolio** | Five to ten near-term apps across different categories, connected by a positive orientation toward lifestyle, connection, community, growth, or useful enjoyment. |
| **Apparel** | A visible, direct use of the Sober Send name and logo on T-shirts, sweatshirts, hats, and potentially other items people can wear proudly. |
| **Community/media** | Website, social channels, stories, content, gatherings, service opportunities, partnerships, and other ways to build connection around the mission. |
| **Future spin-offs** | If a product becomes large enough to merit its own capital, risk profile, team, or strategy, it can be moved into a separate company while maintaining an appropriate relationship to SoberSend. |

### Illustrative structure

```
SOBERSEND
Parent company · lifestyle brand · community · mission
├── App portfolio — Independent product names and sub-brands
│     └── Remember When — Nostalgic trivia game
│     └── Future app 2 / 3 / 4+
├── Apparel — Visible Sober Send wordmark and logo
│     └── T-shirts, sweatshirts, hats
└── Community — Website, social, content, events, service
      └── Stories, meetups, adventures, service
```

Successful products may later spin into separate companies when scale, investment, liability, or focus makes that useful.

### Name presentation

The working corporate and brand name is "SoberSend." The apparel logo may visually render the words as "Sober Send." Final spacing, wordmark, and legal usage should be resolved through design and trademark counsel so the public expression is consistent and protectable.

## 07 · Product & Experience Guardrails

A varied portfolio can still feel coherent when every product reflects the same human purpose.

### What connects the portfolio

SoberSend apps do not all need to be recovery apps. They may span games, lifestyle, wellness, connection, productivity, community, or other categories. Coherence should come from the values behind the products rather than forcing every product to mention sobriety.

### Every product should aim to do at least one of these things well

**Create Connection**  
Help people feel less alone, interact meaningfully, share stories, or enjoy something together.

**Improve Daily Life**  
Make a routine, decision, habit, or experience more useful, positive, engaging, or manageable.

**Encourage Growth**  
Support learning, reflection, curiosity, confidence, or becoming a better version of oneself.

**Invite Participation**  
Prompt action in the world—play, movement, exploration, creativity, service, or shared experience.

**Offer Healthy Enjoyment**  
Provide fun that is constructive, inclusive, and compatible with a sober lifestyle.

**Strengthen Community**  
Create reasons for people to identify, gather, contribute, support one another, or celebrate progress.

### Non-negotiable trust standards

- Do not make unsubstantiated medical, therapeutic, or recovery-outcome claims.
- Do not exploit sensitive recovery stories, relapse, or vulnerability for engagement or sales.
- Protect privacy and be unusually thoughtful about data connected to sobriety, addiction, health, or support networks.
- Avoid mechanics that encourage compulsion, humiliation, exclusion, or unhealthy comparison.
- Make commercial relationships, sponsorships, and affiliate incentives transparent.
- Choose partners whose behavior and products are compatible with the mission and community trust.

### First product test

Remember When is a nostalgic trivia game and the first concrete market test in the portfolio. It demonstrates the intended architecture: the app has its own consumer-facing name and identity, while SoberSend serves primarily as the parent company and publisher behind it.

## 09 · Current Status & Strategic Priorities

What exists today, what is already in motion, and what should happen next.

### Current assets and activity

| Asset | Status |
|-------|--------|
| **Domain** | SoberSend.com is owned by the founder and a website is already live. |
| **Email** | SoberSend@gmail.com is controlled by the founder. |
| **Developer presence** | Developer accounts are associated with the SoberSend email address. |
| **Apps** | Multiple apps are in development or review with Google Play and Apple's App Store, including Remember When. |
| **Apparel identity** | A SoberSend logo is currently in design, with planned use on T-shirts, sweatshirts, and hats. |
| **Company formation** | The founder is evaluating formation of a Washington single-member LLC to serve as the parent entity. |
| **Geographic ambition** | United States first, with a long-term goal of international brand growth. |

### Brand ownership and protection context

The founder is highly committed to the exact name SoberSend. A separate iOS app currently uses the name "SoberSend: Drunk Text Blocker," creating a real trademark-clearance question even though SoberSend's own apps will have different names. The founder is open to counsel recommending direct contact, consent, coexistence, purchase of rights, or acquisition if that becomes the best path.

The initial legal budget is under $1,000, so the working strategy is to do substantial factual research and evidence organization internally, then use attorney time for legal judgment, risk assessment, filing strategy, and negotiation. Outreach has begun to trademark clinics, and an INTA pro bono application is in progress.

### Immediate brand-protection workstream

- Create a dated evidence log showing first use, website publication, app-store submissions, developer-account usage, logo development, and apparel planning.
- Complete federal, state, app-store, domain, social, marketplace, and common-law searches for SoberSend, Sober Send, and similar marks.
- Define the intended goods and services clearly: apparel, downloadable software, potential SaaS, online retail, content, community, and related services.
- Obtain a lawyer's focused opinion on right to use, likelihood of registration, conflict risk, and options involving the existing app owner.
- Resolve whether the protected standard-character mark should be SOBERSEND, SOBER SEND, or both through one application strategy.
- Keep logo and apparel development moving, but avoid irreversible spend until the name receives a reasoned clearance assessment.

### Near-term operating priorities

**Protect the Name**  
Clear the mark, establish an ownership entity, preserve evidence, and define a realistic filing path.

**Launch the First Product**  
Complete store review and use Remember When to learn about development, monetization, support, and distribution.

**Codify the Identity**  
Finish the logo, choose a consistent name presentation, and convert this charter into a practical visual and verbal system.

**Start the Community Carefully**  
Build a social presence and founder story around authentic connection rather than chasing reach before trust.

**Test Apparel**  
Prototype a small number of pieces and validate whether people want to wear the name proudly.

**Build the Portfolio**  
Launch additional independently named products that reflect the positive lifestyle and community guardrails.

## 10 · Strategic Guardrails & Open Decisions

A living charter should make decisions easier while leaving room for discovery.

### What is already clear

- SoberSend is mission-led and community-led, not app-led.
- Recovery is the center of gravity, while the welcome extends to sober-by-choice people and supporters.
- The brand should make sobriety feel proud, social, adventurous, useful, and fully alive.
- The community should help people recover and grow together, with service and role modeling built in.
- Apps will generally use their own names and identities; SoberSend is the parent company and umbrella brand.
- Apparel is a direct and visible expression of the SoberSend name.
- The founder is deeply committed to the name and willing to invest effort in securing it responsibly.

### Open decisions to resolve next

**Legal name**  
Confirm entity name, ownership structure, and whether to form SoberSend LLC before filing any intent-to-use trademark application.

**Wordmark style**  
Choose consistent public usage: SoberSend, Sober Send, or a defined system where one is the legal/word mark and the other is a logo treatment.

**Trademark scope**  
Prioritize the smallest credible set of classes and goods/services that protects the near-term business without overspending.

**Social strategy**  
Decide which platforms matter first, what the founder will share personally, and how community participation will be moderated.

**Apparel launch**  
Determine initial products, production model, quality standard, pricing, and whether proceeds will support any recovery-oriented service work.

**Community model**  
Clarify whether early community is content-led, event-led, service-led, member-led, or some deliberate combination.

**Partnership policy**  
Define which recovery organizations, creators, events, and commercial partners align with the brand—and which do not.

**Impact model**  
Decide how SoberSend will measure contribution beyond revenue: connection, participation, service, reach, or support provided.
